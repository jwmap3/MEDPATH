PARAMEDIC
Clinical Indications:
Venous access when traditional means are unsuccessful and ONLY in those patients with life threatening conditions such as cardiac arrest,
lethal arrhythmias, or in extremis from readily treatable cause.
Contraindications:
Patients where traditional IV access is available.
Notes:
Venous access devices provide direct line into circulation; introduction of air is possible and extremely hazardous. Catheters without anti-
reflux valves may allow blood or air to flow backwards when unclamped and end cap off. Always keep clamps closed except when infusing.
Procedure for Broviac, Hickman, Groshong, Double or Triple Lumen Catheters
 
1.
 Clean the port of the catheter with largest lumen with alcohol wipe.
 
2.
 Using sterile technique, withdraw 5 - 10 mL of blood and place syringe in sharps box.
 
3.
 Using 5 mL of normal saline, access the port with sterile technique and gently attempt to flush the saline.
 
4.
 If there is no resistance, no evidence of infiltration (e.g., no subcutaneous collection of fluid), and no pain experienced by the patient,
then proceed to step 5. If there is resistance, evidence of infiltration, pain experienced by the patient, or any concern that the catheter may be
clotted or dislodged, do not use the catheter.
 
5.
 Begin administration of medications or IV fluids slowly and observe for any signs of infiltration. If difficulties are encountered, stop the
infusion and reassess. Allow fluid to run at KVO rate if shock not present.
 
6.
 Record procedure, any complications, and fluids/medications administered in the Patient Care Report (PCR).
PICC Line (Peripherally Inserted Central Catheter) Procedure
 
1.
 Same procedure as above.
 
2.
 PICC lines are not optimal for rapid fluid resuscitation due to length of catheter and the internal lumen. A pressure bag may augment
fluid flow.

Internal Subcutaneous Infusion Port (Access ONLY with Huber Needle) Procedure
 
1.
 Locate site by visualization and palpation – generally found in upper chest as dome shaped protrusion.
 
2.
 Clean site with alcohol swab.
 
3.
 Insert Huber needle perpendicular to the skin. Attempt to aspirate 10 mL blood with syringe. If unable to aspirate, reclamp catheter and
do not use further.
 
4.
 If successful, flush catheter with 5 - 10 mL normal saline. If catheter does not flush, reclamp and do not use further.
 
5.
 Attach IV and maintain at KVO if shock is not present to prevent clotting of central line.
 
6.
 Record procedure.
Hemodialysis, AV-Fistula and AV-Graft Procedure
Graft = tube that diverts blood flow from artery to vein; Fistula is a surgically created arteriovenous-venous anastomosis. 
Both for hemodialysis. Should NOT be routinely access by EMS
 
1.
 Access must be made using a 14g or 16g IV catheter. Prep site with alcohol swab.
 
2.
 If unsuccessful with access, hold direct pressure over site for 5 - 8 minutes (fistula); 8 - 15 minutes (graft).
Notes:
 A graft is a tube that diverts blood flow from artery to vein and a fistula is a surgically created arteriovenous-venous
anastomosis. Both for hemodialysis. Should NOT be routinely accessed by EMS.
 
3.
 Do not make further attempts to access.
 
4.
 Record procedure.
