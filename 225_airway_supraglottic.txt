PARAMEDIC
Surpaglottic Airway
Clinical Indications:
Inability to secure an endotracheal tube in a patient who does not have a gag reflex where at least one failed intubation attempt has occurred.
Appropriate intubation is impossible due to patient access or difficult airway anatomy.
Absolute Contraindications:
Deforming facial trauma. 
Procedure
 
1. 
Prepare, position and oxygenate the patient with 100% Oxygen.
 
2.
 Choose SGA Airway size per package recommendations based on patient weight.
 
3.
 Lubricate SGA Airway with a water-soluble jelly.
 
4.
 Position head in sniffing position if C-spine injury not suspected.
 
5.
 Hold in dominant hand and advance into position until seated on the glottic opening.
 
6.
 Never forced tube.
 
7.
 Attach BVM, gently ventilate and simultaneously withdraw airway until ventilation is easy (i.e., large tidal volume with minimum
pressure).
 
8.
 Confirm placement with auscultation, end-tidal CO2 (capnometry); secure tube in position. A Paramedic may insert up to an 18 Fr
diameter gastric tube through the appropriate SGA Airway port for gastric decompression if indicated.
 
9.
 During transport, place cervical collar to prevent accidental displacement of the tube. For non-trauma, you may remove the collar upon
arrival at your destination.
