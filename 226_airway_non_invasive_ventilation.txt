PARAMEDIC
Clinical Indications:
NIV should be applied to patients in whom inadequate ventilation is suspected. CPAP may be utilized in patients with suspected acute
pulmonary edema, asthma, or COPD. CHF and/or acute pulmonary edema should be treated with 10 - 15 cmH20 CPAP whereas COPD or
asthma patients should have 5 cm H2O CPAP.
CPAP may be utilized for patients with respiratory distress.  
COPD and Asthma should start with 5 cm H20 CPAP; 
Acute pulmonary edema should have 5, 7.5, 10 cm H20 CPAP
If transport ventilator available (not Autovent)
May utilize ventilator to deliver CPAP with same CPAP settings as disposable CPAP
Contraindications:
Patients requiring a secure airway (i.e., endotracheal intubation). 
Patient's who are actively vomiting, suspected bowel obstruction, patients who cannot maintain their airway
Procedure
 
1. 
Ensure adequate oxygen supply to ventilation device.
 
2.
 Explain the procedure to the patient.
 
3.
 Consider placement of a nasopharyngeal airway. Apply Capnography.
 
4.
 Place the delivery mask over the mouth and nose. Oxygen should be flowing at this point.
 
5.
 Secure the mask with provided straps starting with the lower straps until minimal air leak occurs.
 
6.
 Evaluate the response by the patient. Assess breath sounds, oxygen saturation, and general appearance of the patient.
 
7.
 Encourage the patient to allow forced ventilation occur. Observe closely for signs of complication. The patient must be breathing
spontaneously and must not have an alteration in mental status for CPAP to work.
 
8.
 Document time and response on Patient Care Report (PCR).
