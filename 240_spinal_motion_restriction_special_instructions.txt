PARAMEDIC
Procedure
 
1. 
 Patients with difficulty breathing: exercise caution when applying SMR to these patients. Position appropriately.
 
2.
 Pediatrics: avoid movements that provoke spinal motion.
 
3.
 Combative patients: avoid interactions that provoke increased spinal motion or agitation.
 
4.
 SMR should reduce patient discomfort. SMR that increases pain should be avoided.
 
5.
 The goal of SMR is to prevent further spinal injury. A patient's cervical spine should never be moved if movement increases pain,
neurologic deficits or spasm.
 
6.
 SMR tools include: Vacuum splints, cervical collars, scoop stretchers, long boards, straps, head immobilization devices, tape and padding
(pillows/sheets)
 
7.
 Alert, neurologically intact penetrating thoracic trauma victims without spinal pain do not need immobilization.
 
8.
 Spinal immobilization should be reserved primarily for patients who have received a high impact with multiple blunt trauma.
 
9.
 Allow patients to self extricate when possible with assistance. No standing backboard take-downs.
 
10.
 For a patient without midline neck/spine tenderness, ALOC, intoxication or distracting injury, SMR should be withheld as long as
patient can be accurately evaluated.
