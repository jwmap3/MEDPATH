PARAMEDIC
Clinical Indications:
Apply SMR to any patient identified to have a potential spine injury that might benefit. 
Patients warranting SMR:
Cervical, thoracic or lumbar pain/tenderness.
Signs or symptoms of neurologic deficit.
High Risk Mechanism (axial load, sudden deceleration, lateral force bend, penetrating with spine involvement).
Poor communication (altered LOC, pediatrics, language barrier, unreliable interaction).
The following is a list of acceptable methods for SMR frown least invasive to most invasive:
Fowlers, Semi-Fowlers, supine on gurney with cervical collar only.
Supine position with vacuum mattress device splinting head-to-toe.
Child car seat with padding.
Supine on scoop stretcher, secured with straps, padding, head blocks – avoid log roll.
Supine positioning with long backboard, straps, padding, head blocks or full-body vacuum splint.
Procedure
 
1.
 Provide manual stabilization to restrict gross head movement. Alert, cooperative sober patients may be allowed to self-limit movement
with or without a cervical collar, especially if ambulating on arrival.
 
2.
 Place appropriately sized collar.
 
3.
 Obtain focused history and examination for complaints of pain, numbness, tingling, GCS, neuro deficits, spine tenderness, deformity, or
painful distracting injury.
 
4.
 Extricate patient limiting flexion, extension, rotation, distraction of spine. Use pull sheets, scoop stretchers as needed. Long backboards
should have limited utilization; Utilize full-body vacuum splint if available.
 
5.
 If patient is to be transported on hard device, apply adequate padding to prevent tissue ischemia and provide comfort.
 
6.
 Place patient in best position to protect airway.
 
7.
 Repeat neurological exam.
 
8.
 Use SpO2/ETCO2 to monitor respiratory function.
 
9.
 Document exam findings in PRC.
